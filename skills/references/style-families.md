# Y2K Style Families

Use this reference only after inspecting the sources. Pick exactly one primary family and at most one supporting family. The family is not a preset: it is combined with a source-responsive future premise, spatial stage, material system, and emotional tension.

## Style Construction Axes

Resolve these before writing the prompt:

### Future Premise

Write one sentence describing the imagined world and the subject's place inside it. It should feel like a future imagined near the turn of the millennium, not a forecast made today. Good premises contain a subject, a technological condition, a space, and a tension.

Examples:

- The subject waits alone inside a networked transit terminal imagined in 2001.
- The portrait is presented as a prototype identity being calibrated by a friendly but intrusive machine.
- An ordinary source prop becomes the hero product in an optimistic transparent-tech advertisement.
- A personal photograph survives as a memory fragment inside an abandoned desktop system.

### Spatial Stage

Choose one dominant stage:

- **Flat editorial field:** poster paper, magazine page, xerox wall, or sticker plane.
- **Desktop/interface space:** overlapping windows, camera or player frames, diagnostic panels, or virtual work surface.
- **Nocturnal city/transit:** platform, tunnel, street, rooftop, elevator, reflective corridor, or infrastructure threshold.
- **Synthetic laboratory:** scan chamber, identity station, clean-room surface, data clinic, or specimen display.
- **Product/industrial void:** catalog stage, modular enclosure, prototype showroom, aerospace bay, or blank engineered environment.
- **Cosmic/atmospheric zone:** horizonless light, orbital diagram, artificial sky, weightless haze, or signal field.
- **Bio-tech habitat:** artificial water, membrane garden, synthetic ecology, submerged chamber, or living interface.

### Dominant Material System

Choose one dominant material and at most one accent:

- paper, ink, toner, tape, and fibrous edges;
- CRT glow, pixels, scan lines, compression, and translucent screen overlays;
- liquid chrome, mirror metal, iridescent film, and high-specular droplets;
- glass, acrylic, clear housings, fiber optics, bubbles, and luminous edges;
- inflated plastic, jelly, silicone, vinyl, and soft translucent membranes;
- wet asphalt, reflective tile, mist, flare, and emissive atmospheric light;
- brushed metal, molded shells, vents, cables, ports, and engineered seams.

Keep light, shadow, transparency, reflection, edge quality, and type treatment consistent with this choice.

### Emotional Tension

Choose one polarity and let composition express it:

- optimism / unease;
- connection / isolation;
- intimacy / surveillance;
- organic / synthetic;
- play / control;
- human identity / machine classification;
- nostalgia / unrealized future;
- speed / suspension;
- abundance / emptiness.

## Routing Overview

| Family | Strong source cues | Dominant world |
|---|---|---|
| Pop scrapbook | group, fashion, full-body pose, performance, names | saturated editorial collage |
| Webcore desktop | selfie, screen, camera, phone, casual diary | early-internet interface |
| Punk halftone | dark fashion, performance, contrast, close details | confrontational print zine |
| Liquid chrome futurism | jewelry, reflective props, fluid pose, sleek silhouette | unstable machine-organic portal |
| Techno pop campaign | clear promotional portrait, expressive pose, fashion, gadgets, product-like props | imagined turn-of-the-millennium consumer-tech campaign |

## 1. Pop Scrapbook

Use when the sources contain group shots, full-body fashion, performance, bold posing, collectible-card energy, or a need for labels and names.

- **Composition:** one dominant cutout or group, small portrait cards, diagonal overlap, outlined title, sticker-like props, generous negative gaps between dense clusters.
- **Source transformations:** clean cutout, monochrome portrait card, halftone echo, silhouette, accessory close-up.
- **Palette:** saturated blue, pink, red, lime, or orange with black, white, and one neutral paper tone.
- **Type:** outlined display lettering, rounded label capsules, condensed sans, compact name tags.
- **Best stage/material:** flat editorial field; paper, ink, plastic sticker, or one chrome accent.
- **Avoid:** equal-size idol grid, every face outlined identically, decorative labels without hierarchy, or turning the poster into generic fan merchandise.

## 2. Webcore Desktop

Use when the sources contain selfies, cameras, phones, computers, screens, casual snapshots, personal diary energy, or when the subject benefits from virtual framing.

- **Composition:** hero image inside or crossing a chunky window, nested camera/player replay, cursor path, small status panels, one controlled stack of overlapping windows.
- **Source transformations:** pixelated thumbnail, low-resolution replay, scan crop, selection box, webcam frame, compressed ghost.
- **Palette:** Windows-era blue, cool grey, white, cyan, acid green, or warning red; use screen glow selectively.
- **Type:** pixel sans, bitmap labels, compact system copy, short lowercase diary phrase.
- **Best stage/material:** desktop/interface space; CRT glow, pixels, translucent screen layers.
- **Avoid:** contemporary glassmorphism, clean SaaS dashboards, exact branded software replicas, or interface chrome that buries the subject.

## 3. Punk Halftone

Use when the sources contain dark fashion, performance, harsh contrast, expressive close-ups, confrontational posture, or print-zine energy.

- **Composition:** overscale monochrome face or body fragment, hard rectangular crops, one sharp diagonal, dense type block against a quieter black or paper field.
- **Source transformations:** duotone, coarse halftone, xerox contrast, registration shift, torn cutout, ink silhouette.
- **Palette:** black, off-white, electric blue, ultraviolet, dirty red, or acid yellow; keep the system narrow.
- **Type:** condensed grotesk, rough serif, blackletter accent, stamped mono, or cut-and-paste type.
- **Best stage/material:** flat editorial field; toner, ink, tape, coarse paper.
- **Avoid:** distressing every element equally, unreadable pseudo-copy, horror mutilation, or accidental heavy-metal pastiche.

## 4. Liquid Chrome Futurism

Use when the sources contain reflective accessories, jewelry, fluid hair or pose, sleek objects, curved silhouettes, beauty lighting, or a desire for machine-organic sensuality.

- **Composition:** a clear fidelity hero interrupted by one biomorphic chrome portal, reflective ribbon, liquid frame, or floating metallic mass; echoes appear as reflections or insets rather than random duplicates.
- **Source transformations:** mirror-edged cutout, chrome contour, warped reflective replay, iridescent crop, silver silhouette.
- **Palette:** silver, graphite, icy cyan, electric blue, violet shadow, and one hot accent such as magenta or signal orange.
- **Type:** stretched techno sans, polished bevel accent, narrow extended grotesk, or liquid-metal title used sparingly.
- **Best stage/material:** product void, cosmic zone, or synthetic lab; liquid chrome as dominant material with glass as accent.
- **Avoid:** random 3D blobs, luxury-logo styling, physically inconsistent reflections, covering the identity anchor, or mistaking metallic decoration for composition.

## 5. Techno Pop Campaign

Use when the sources contain a clear promotional portrait, expressive full-body pose, fashion styling, consumer electronics, toys, game-related objects, product-like props, bold color cues, or a subject that can plausibly become the star of an imagined turn-of-the-millennium technology campaign.

- **Composition:** build one product-like world around a clear promotional hero. Turn one oversized source-responsive gadget, product shell, screen, control surface, aquarium, speaker, camera, console, or synthetic display structure into the spatial stage. Add one to three smaller portrait replays only when they demonstrate pose, clothing, scale, or campaign narrative. Let the hero sit on, lean against, emerge from, overlap, or appear inside the product structure so the device functions as architecture rather than a detached sticker.
- **Source transformations:** high-contrast promotional hero, hard-flash portrait, slightly overexposed highlight treatment, saturated beauty crop, product-screen replay, catalog-scale full-body cutout, low-resolution advertisement echo, translucent-plastic inset, or restrained early-digital sharpening. Keep one unmistakably source-faithful face and preserve which face, hair, body, clothing, pose, expression, and prop belong together.
- **Portrait treatment:** heighten facial and hair contrast, deepen crisp dark features, slightly increase skin, lip, cheek, eye-makeup, and clothing saturation, and allow controlled highlight bloom or mild overexposure on the forehead, nose bridge, cheekbones, shoulders, or other flash-facing planes. Preserve visible facial structure and enough natural texture for recognition. The portrait must feel deliberately processed rather than untouched, but never illustrated, melted, excessively smoothed, recolored beyond recognition, or replaced by a generic plastic face. Apply stronger color shift, halftone, bloom, compression, or edge light only to smaller echoes.
- **Palette:** choose one dominant high-saturation campaign or product color, one strong contrasting dark, and white, silver, grey, or transparent plastic as the engineered neutral. Repeat the dominant color through the background, device surfaces, title, portrait grading, and small controls without erasing source-specific skin, clothing, or prop colors.
- **Material and reproduction:** use molded plastic, translucent acrylic, glossy product shells, synthetic glass, soft rubber controls, low-cost metallic effects, screen glow, hard flash, scan texture, print dots, compression noise, or imperfect early-digital cutout edges. Favor an appealing but visibly artificial 1998–2010 entertainment-advertising finish over seamless contemporary 3D product rendering.
- **Type:** use one short game-cover, product-name, or entertainment-campaign title as a major secondary focal point. Favor wide, italic, inflated, outlined, beveled, winged, star-framed, or speed-line display lettering with controlled shadow or synthetic surface effects. Keep wording legible and generic; do not reproduce real logos, brand marks, artist identities, product claims, or the exact letterforms and title construction of a reference image.
- **Best stage/material:** product or industrial void, synthetic showroom, gadget interior, entertainment display, or artificial consumer habitat; molded plastic, acrylic, screen glass, soft rubber, or low-cost metallic finish as the dominant material, with early-digital print and display artifacts as reproduction behavior.
- **Avoid:** a natural portrait pasted unchanged onto a decorative background, generic beauty retouching, excessive skin smoothing, blown highlights that erase facial features, saturation that changes identity, unrelated gadget stickers, a flat product catalog with no human–device spatial interaction, contemporary luxury advertising, clean modern UI, photoreal blockbuster product visualization, copied game or music logos, or allowing the device and title to overpower the recognizable hero.

## Supporting-Family Rules

The supporting family contributes only one subsystem and should occupy roughly 20–35% of the visible grammar:

- **Material support:** for example Pop Scrapbook with one restrained Liquid Chrome accent.
- **Interface support:** for example Pop Scrapbook with one Webcore camera/player frame.
- **Print support:** for example Liquid Chrome Futurism with Punk Halftone background reproduction, while the hero and world remain chrome-led.
- **Editorial support:** for example Webcore Desktop with Punk Halftone treatment limited to thumbnails or status panels.
- **Campaign support:** for example Pop Scrapbook with Techno Pop Campaign limited to one oversized product stage and one game-cover title, while the paper-editorial hierarchy remains dominant.
- **Product-interface support:** for example Techno Pop Campaign with Webcore Desktop limited to one screen replay or compact control panel inside the oversized device.

Useful pairings include Pop + Webcore, Pop + Liquid Chrome, Liquid Chrome + Punk, Webcore + Punk, Techno Pop Campaign + Webcore, and Techno Pop Campaign + Pop.

When Techno Pop Campaign is primary, keep the oversized consumer device, promotional portrait treatment, product-color system, and campaign-title hierarchy dominant. Borrow Webcore only for a contained screen or control subsystem, Pop only for a small editorial card or playful label layer, Punk only for restrained reproduction texture, or Liquid Chrome only for one title or hardware accent. Do not combine it with another family that also attempts to control the entire world, portrait treatment, palette, and headline.

Do not pair families merely for variety. Reject a pairing when both families compete to control the same composition, world, palette, type, and material behavior. If the result cannot be described as one primary world with one borrowed subsystem, use only the primary family.
